If you're always running late

You've tried leaving earlier. It didn't work.

You leave what feels like plenty of time, and you're somehow still late.

You open one app to check one thing, and forty minutes are gone.

You set the alarms, then turn them off without moving an inch.

Chronic lateness usually isn't a discipline problem — it's how time gets away from you in the last ten minutes before leaving. Reminders don't fix that. Enforcement does.

What it does

More than a blocker on a timer.

Live leave-by

It watches the traffic for you

Outset recalculates your leave-by time as conditions change. When the roads slow down, it moves your deadline earlier and shields your apps sooner — before you're already behind.

Geofence unlock

Leaving is the only key

No timer to outlast, no code to enter, no sticker to tap. The apps stay dark until you physically cross the edge of your home area. Then they come back on their own.

On-time record

It keeps score

Every on-time departure builds your streak. It turns "leave on time" from a vague intention into a running record you can actually see — and don't want to break.

Calendar-aware

It knows where you're due

Connect your calendar and Outset can pull your next event, set the destination, and arm itself — so the mornings you most need it don't depend on you remembering to set it up.

Why not the others

Every other approach leaves the same gap.

The tools already out there each stop one step short of the thing that actually gets you moving.

Reminder apps
Tell you it's time. You swipe the notification away and keep scrolling.
Timer blockers
Make you wait out a countdown. The clock runs whether or not you've actually left.
Tag blockers
Unlock when you walk to a QR or NFC sticker. So you walk over, unlock, and sit back down.
Outset
Unlocks only when you physically leave. The one thing it rewards is the one thing you're trying to do.

Privacy

Most screen-time apps make money from your data. Outset is built so there's nothing to sell.

No account, ever

No sign-up, no login, no profile. You open the app and use it. There's no you-shaped record for us to hold.

No servers of ours

We don't run a backend. Your schedules, location, and records never reach us — because there's nowhere for them to go.

Your data, your iCloud

History syncs across your devices through your own iCloud, under Apple's control. Even the apps you block are opaque tokens we can't read.

Questions

The things people ask first.

What if there's an emergency?

Outset only shields the distracting apps you pick. Phone calls, messages, maps, and everything else on your phone keep working normally.

Will it drain my battery?

Departure detection is built on Apple's native geofencing — the same power-efficient location tech iOS already runs in the background — rather than keeping GPS on the whole time.

Which apps can it block?

Any apps you choose, through Apple's own Screen Time picker. Instagram, X, games, whatever eats your mornings — you decide the list.

Does it need signal to work?

Detecting that you've left home works offline. Live traffic — the part that adjusts your leave-by time — needs a connection to recalculate.

Is it iPhone only?

For now, yes — iPhone on iOS 17 or later. The app relies on Apple's Screen Time and geofencing frameworks, which are iOS-specific.

What will it cost?

Outset will be free to start, with optional paid features for people who want more. No account required either way. Final pricing lands at launch.
